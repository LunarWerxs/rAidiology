# Model card: SuprSkan disc grader v1

Generated by `pipeline/eval/model_card.py` from the model's `grader.json` and `pipeline/eval/SCORECARD.md`.

**TSS weights: permission pending.** The grader's crops are centred with TotalSpineSeg's disc labels, whose released weights NeuroPoly has not yet cleared for commercial use. Nothing that depends on them goes into a paid product until they do (`LICENSING.md`).

## What it does

Grades one lumbar disc (T12-L1 to L5-S1) from a sagittal MRI: Pfirrmann grade 1-5 and the probability of herniation, narrowing, bulging, spondylolisthesis, upper and lower endplate change and any Modic change. It is a research tool for a second look, not a diagnosis, and not a medical device.

## Data and licences

- Trained on SPIDER (Zenodo 10159290, CC BY 4.0) development patients only (168 patients, 1004 discs located by TotalSpineSeg); the 44 held-out test patients were never used for training, calibration or any choice.
- The truth is one musculoskeletal radiologist's grading of a Dutch back-pain cohort.
- Trained from scratch: no pretrained backbone (ImageNet terms are non-commercial).
- Disc localisation: TotalSpineSeg r20260730 disc labels (TSS weights: permission pending).

## Inputs and model

- Inputs: crops.py: T2+T1, 5 planes x 80 x 112 at 0.6 mm, 3.3 mm apart; T1 missing -> zero channel plus a flag; each series scaled by its 1st/99th percentiles.
- Network: four convolutional stages, an ordinal head (four cumulative logits) for Pfirrmann and one sigmoid logit per binary item. Pfirrmann = 1 + the number of cumulative probabilities above 0.5.
- Training settings: `{"epochs": 60, "batch": 32, "lr": 0.002, "weight_decay": 0.0001, "patience": 10, "width": 32, "early_stop_frac": 0.15}`.
- Five fold models (5-fold cross-validation by patient); the shipped model averages their logits.

## Limits

- Lumbar only; cervical levels keep SuprSkan's reading and measurements.
- Scores on SPIDER are optimistic for localisation: TotalSpineSeg was trained on SPIDER's images.
- One radiologist's grades on one country's back-pain patients; other readers and populations differ.
