See the pictures from your own scan, at home.

The hospital gave you a disc, or a ZIP from the patient portal. Open it here and look through your MRI, CT or X-ray in your own browser. There is no account, and your scan never leaves your computer unless you choose to donate it.

Open your own scan Try a sample scan first

When you choose your scan, your browser asks whether this page may read the files. Allowing it only lets the page read them; nothing is sent.

On a phone? A ZIP from your patient portal opens here too. A disc needs a computer, so email yourself the link for when you are at one.

What it does not do yet: it does not say what your own scan shows, or explain your written report. Today it shows you the pictures, and on the sample scan it names each part of the body you point at. The rest is still being built and tested. It never says whether anything is wrong.

How it works

  1. 1

    Get your scan files

    Put the hospital's CD or DVD in a computer, or find the ZIP you downloaded from the patient portal. A phone cannot read a disc, so use a computer for that.

  2. 2

    Choose the disc or the ZIP

    Press Open your own scan and pick the disc, or the ZIP. Your browser reads the files where they are. They do not go anywhere.

  3. 3

    Look through the pictures

    A scan is several sets of pictures taken from different directions. Pick a set, scroll through it, zoom in, and change the brightness.

No disc to hand? Try a sample scan to see what it looks like before you find yours.

What it does today, and what it does not

What it does today

  • Opens the disc or ZIP your hospital gave you.
  • Shows every picture, with zoom, brightness and contrast.
  • Runs in your browser on your own computer. Nothing is uploaded unless you donate a scan.
  • On the sample scan, names each organ, bone and blood vessel you point at.

What it does not do

  • It does not say what your own scan shows, or explain your written report, yet.
  • It is not a diagnosis and it is not a doctor.
  • It does not replace your radiologist's report.
  • It is not cleared or approved by any regulator.

What is being built

Alongside the viewer, rAidiology trains research models on public scans and publishes how each one scored. On this site, only the sample scan has labels. This is where the models stand.

  • Spine MRIA research model that grades the wear of each disc (in development, not on this site). It was compared with one radiologist's grading on scans it had not seen.
  • X-rayA research model that scores X-rays for a visible broken bone. Tested on 4,170 children's wrist X-rays it had not seen before.
  • CTA research model that outlines 117 organs and bones. Tested against experts' outlines on 89 scans it had not seen.

None of these is a diagnosis or a medical device. Each model is published with how often it was right on scans it never saw during training.

Your scan stays yours

A medical scan is about as private as a thing gets. So the page that opens it sends nothing unless you choose to donate a scan.

  • Nothing is uploaded unless you choose to donate a scan. The page reads the files on your own computer and draws them on your screen. Close the tab and they are gone from it.
  • Your name is never read. The files carry your name, your date of birth and your hospital number. The page skips your name and number. It reads your date of birth on your device only, to check you are 18 or older if you donate, and never sends it.
  • No trackers. No analytics, no cookies on the pages you use, and nothing loaded from anybody else.
  • You can check it yourself. Go to the Open your own scan page first. Then switch off your Wi-Fi, and only then choose your scan. It still opens, because your scan never needed to go anywhere.
  • One thing your browser may say. When you pick a folder, your browser asks whether the page may read it, and some browsers word that as "Upload files to this site?". Nothing is sent.
