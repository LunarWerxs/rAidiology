# Model card: rAidiology CT segmenter v1

Generated by `modalities/ct/eval/model_card.py` from the model's `meta.json`, `modalities/ct/eval/split.json`, the scorer's cells and the run ledger. No number in it was typed by hand.

**Not a medical device.** It labels anatomy for a picture and for measurements; it is not a diagnosis and it does not look for disease (`LICENSING.md`).

## What it answers

One question, fixed before any model was trained: **where is each organ and bone in this scan?** One label per voxel over the 117 structures the TotalSegmentator dataset marks. It does not say whether any of them is healthy.

## What it was trained on

- Wasserthal, Breit, Meyer, Pradella, Hinck, Sauter, Heye, Boll, Cyriac, Yang, Bach, Segeroth, 'TotalSegmentator: Robust Segmentation of 104 Anatomic Structures in CT Images', Radiology: Artificial Intelligence 5(5) (2023), doi:10.1148/ryai.230024; dataset v2.0.1 doi:10.5281/zenodo.10047292, CC BY 4.0
- 1139 development scans in 5 folds; 89 held-out test scans, the dataset authors' own test split, never used for training or for any choice.
- **Trained from scratch, with no pretrained weights of any kind.** No ImageNet, no model zoo: every weight was learned from those CC BY 4.0 scans, so the model carries no 'permission pending' and the attribution above is what the licence asks for.
- The dataset publishes no patient identifier, so the scan is the unit and two scans of one person could fall on both sides. This is on the scorecard.

## Model

- A 3D U-Net, widths 32 - 64 - 128 - 256 - 320, two 3 x 3 x 3 convolutions a stage, normalised per scan.
- It reads the scan at 3 mm (each 2 x 2 x 2 block of the 1.5 mm scan averaged) in cubes of 128 voxels, and its probabilities are interpolated back to 1.5 mm before a label is chosen. Every score below is taken at 1.5 mm, against the expert masks as published.
- 5 fold models; each development score comes from the model that never saw that scan, and the test split is read by all of them, their probabilities averaged.
- A fixed schedule a fold (6000 steps at 6 mm, then 3500 steps at 3 mm): nothing was stopped early or chosen by looking at held-out scans.
- Training settings: `{"clip": 12.0, "curve_every": 1500, "curve_scans": 16, "deep": [1.0, 0.5, 0.25], "ema": 0.999, "gain": 0.1, "loaders": 3, "offset": 0.1, "patch": 128, "phases": [{"batch": 2, "lr": 0.001, "mm": 6, "steps": 6000}, {"batch": 1, "lr": 0.0005, "mm": 3, "steps": 3500}], "structure_share": 0.5, "warmup": 0.04, "warp_rotate": 0.15, "warp_scale": [0.85, 1.2], "warp_share": 0.3, "weight_decay": 3e-05, "widths": [32, 64, 128, 256, 320]}`.

## What to hold against it

- **The scan is the unit, not the patient.** The dataset publishes no patient identifier, so every interval above treats two scans of one person as independent.
- **One dataset.** Most of its scans come from one institute and one manufacturer; the strata above are the only evidence about anything else, and they are small.
- **Measured at 1.5 mm only.** A scan at another spacing is resampled, and that path has no scorecard behind it; the reading says so when it happens.
- **It invents structures.** The last column above is how often it outlines a structure the scan does not contain. Dice never sees that, which is why it has its own column.
- **The working grid has a ceiling.** Labels at 3 mm, reproduced perfectly, would score 0.8837 mean Dice at 1.5 mm. The model interpolates probabilities, so it is not strictly bound by that figure, but thin structures pay for the coarse grid.
- **A perfect answer scores under 1.** The expert masks overlap in 0.15% of marked voxels, and this model gives one label per voxel.
- **Per structure, not just on average.** `eval/SCORECARD.md` lists all 117 structures; the weakest are the ones to distrust in a picture.

## Running it

    python modalities/ct/eval/segmenter.py scan <ct.nii.gz> --out labels.nii.gz

Weights: `Models/ct-segmenter-v1/` beside the repository (5 files, checksums in `meta.json`).
A test scan took 7.9 s on the graphics card it was trained on, all fold models included. Its cost on a processor has not been measured.
