# Model card: rAidiology X-ray fracture detector v1

Generated by `modalities/xray/eval/model_card.py` from the model's `meta.json`, `modalities/xray/eval/split.json`, the two datasets' own index files and `modalities/xray/eval/SCORECARD.md`. No number in it was typed by hand.

**Not a medical device.** It is a research tool, not a diagnosis (`LICENSING.md`).

## What it answers

One question per radiograph, fixed before any model was trained: **does this radiograph show a fracture?** The output is a score from 0 to 1 per image, where higher means a fracture is more likely, and a yes or no from that score at a threshold fitted on development folds only. It does not say where the fracture is, what kind it is, or how bad.

## What it was trained on

Development images only, from `fracatlas` and `grazpedwri`. The held-out test images were never used for training, the threshold, or any choice.

## The split

Seed 20261001, 20% of the units held out as test, the rest in 5 folds, written once and committed (`split.json`). The rule, as recorded there:

> The locked split for both X-ray datasets: 20% of the units held out as test, 80% development in
> 5 folds. Seed 20261001. Written once, committed, and never regenerated: `split.json` is the record.
>
> The unit is the thing resampled and the thing that may not appear on both sides:
> - `grazpedwri`: the **patient** (`patient_id`), so a person's images never straddle the split.
> - `fracatlas`: the **image**, because FracAtlas publishes no patient identifier. Two radiographs of
>   the same person could therefore fall on both sides there. That limitation is on the scorecard and
>   is why the ship decision rests on GRAZPEDWRI-DX.
>
> Units are stratified by facts taken from the dataset's own index (`datasets.index`), read off the
> unit's first image in key order:
> - `grazpedwri`: age band x sex x whether any of the patient's images shows a fracture;
> - `fracatlas`: body part x view x whether the image shows a fracture.
> Strata with fewer than 10 units are pooled into one "pooled small strata" stratum.
>
> Procedure (deterministic, one numpy Generator per dataset): for each stratum in sorted name order,
> the stratum's units sorted by key (numerically when every key is an integer, else as strings) are
> permuted; the first round(0.2 * n) go to test; the rest of every stratum, concatenated in that same
> order, are dealt to folds 0..4 in turn with one running counter, so small strata spread over folds.

- GRAZPEDWRI-DX is split by **patient**, so a child's images never fall on both sides.
- FracAtlas is split by **image (no patient identifier is published)**: FracAtlas publishes no patient identifier, so two radiographs of the same person could fall on both sides. That is why the ship decision rests on GRAZPEDWRI-DX and FracAtlas is an external check.

## Shortcuts it could learn instead of the fracture

- **A cast.** 5776 GRAZPEDWRI-DX images show a plaster cast, which almost always means a fracture somebody already diagnosed.
- **A follow-up.** Only 10861 of the 20327 images are initial examinations; the rest are check-ups of a known fracture.
- **An unsure label.** 537 images are flagged 'diagnosis uncertain'; none were thrown away.

What the pre-declared subsets showed (GRAZPEDWRI-DX, development, out of fold):

- Initial examinations only, so no follow-up of a known fracture (8689 images): AUROC 0.953 (95% CI 0.947 to 0.958), -0.012 against all images.
- Images with no cast (11641 images): AUROC 0.962 (95% CI 0.958 to 0.966), -0.003 against all images.
- Images whose diagnosis is not flagged uncertain (15723 images): AUROC 0.968 (95% CI 0.964 to 0.971), +0.003 against all images.

And on the held-out test:

- Initial examinations only, so no follow-up of a known fracture (2172 images): AUROC 0.967 (95% CI 0.957 to 0.976), -0.009 against all images.
- Images with no cast (2910 images): AUROC 0.971 (95% CI 0.963 to 0.978), -0.005 against all images.
- Images whose diagnosis is not flagged uncertain (4067 images): AUROC 0.977 (95% CI 0.971 to 0.983), +0.001 against all images.

A subset AUROC well below the all-images AUROC would mean part of the score came from the shortcut, not the break.

## Who it is NOT for

- It is paediatric-wrist-heavy: 20327 of the 24410 training-pool images are GRAZPEDWRI-DX children's wrists. An adult hip is out of distribution (FracAtlas has only 338 hip images in total), and so is any body part neither dataset covers, such as the skull, spine, ribs or pelvis.
- Not for anyone deciding on treatment: a radiologist's report decides, not this.
- Not for images other than plain radiographs (no CT, MRI or ultrasound), or for photographs of a screen.
- Not a medical device.
