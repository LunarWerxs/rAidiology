## Not a medical device

rAidiology is not cleared or approved by the FDA or any other regulator. It is for education and a
second look, not for diagnosis or treatment decisions, and a commercial licence does not change
that.

- **In the US:** software that analyses medical images for a medical purpose is generally
  regulated as a device: the exemption for clinical decision support software excludes functions that "acquire, process, or analyze a
  medical image" (FD&C Act section 520(o)(1)(E)).
- **Even for patients:** what counts as a device depends on the intended use and the claims made,
  so a patient-facing "second look" can still be regulated if it is marketed as diagnosis.
- **Elsewhere:** other markets have their own rules, for example the EU Medical Device Regulation.

Selling it for diagnosis needs regulatory clearance first.

## Public datasets approved for download, 2026-10-04

The data hunt ([docs/research/data-hunt-2026-10-04.md](docs/research/data-hunt-2026-10-04.md)) read each licence at
its source. The owner then approved the download in chat on 2026-10-04 ("Download anything and everything that works
best for all of us"), with three rulings:

- **The dataset's own website licence governs:** "if they have a license on their website, we would go with that".
  That is why TBX11K is approved: its lab's page says CC BY 4.0.
- **Share-alike is acceptable:** "I would probably assume share alike is okay". CC BY-SA sets are approved. Any
  adapted copy of their data that we share goes out under the same licence.
- **Questionable sets are noted, not fetched:** these are the report's CHECK rows, where a licence conflicts or a
  term is unclear. One is decided case by case only if it would improve a model significantly.

A fourth ruling followed on 2026-10-05:

- **The download record's licence governs, and the owner is not asked again:** "the download record, if it is...
  has a license that we can use. Consider the download record the one we should use. And don't ask again." When the
  record a set is downloaded from (its Zenodo, figshare, Mendeley Data, Dryad or TCIA record) carries a licence that
  allows commercial use, that licence decides, whatever the paper, the record's own description, a README or another
  site says. A conflict of that kind is no longer a CHECK and is no longer flagged to the owner. The ruling does not
  reach a record whose own licence is non-commercial, pictures a record re-shares from someone else's non-commercial
  collection, or files behind a login, request or bot check.

A fifth ruling followed on 2026-10-06:

- **No email goes to a data owner, and every ask already sent counts as a no:** "Most of the emails you sent out requesting information about datasets in order to use them commercially pretty much responded and said no. So stop sending emails. I assume they're all no." The nine
  asks of 2026-10-05 (MIDRC, CSAW-CC, NIAID TB Portals, BIMCV for PadChest, RSNA, CURVAS, Segmed, NCI CDAS for
  PLCO and NLST, Deepwise for ChestX-Det10) are refusals: what each asked for (MIDRC's data under its
  agreement, CSAW-CC's files, TB Portals, PadChest, the unlicensed RSNA challenge sets, CURVAS, PLCO, NLST's
  clinical data, ChestX-Det10's boxes) is not downloaded or used, and Segmed gets no purchase. What the same
  makers publish elsewhere under a licence of its own keeps its own row: NLST's CC BY 4.0 images, RSNA
  Pneumonia on RSNA's own terms, and ChestX-Det (Deepwise's separate Apache-2.0 repository). NeuroPoly was never asked, so the TotalSpineSeg weights and the PAM50 template stay
  permission pending for good (Plan B, above). A set comes in only on its own published licence.

**Too big for their value, so not fetched:** FDG-PET-CT-Lesions v2 (defaced) (419 GB of whole-body PET/CT for a value of 1); COVID-19-NY-SBU (TCIA) v1 (511 GB of COVID-era chest CT and X-ray for a value of 1) (since 2026-10-05 held as training copies instead, about 15 times smaller; see the last section); VICTRE in-silico trial (TCIA) v1 (1.03 TB of computer-made (in-silico) breast images for a value of 1); Advanced-MRI-Breast-Lesions v2 (646 GB of breast MRI for a value of 2). ## Training copies of big collections, 2026-10-05

The owner, 2026-10-05: "A non-regressive. Compression algorithm. That we can still use the data. To train the...
model, but store the data at, like, you know, significantly, borderline 90% smaller."

These collections are stored only as training copies, made by `tools/train_copy.py`. Each series comes from IDC's
public AWS bucket, and every object is checked by its md5. CT is resampled to 1.5 mm on each axis finer than that (the CT
models read 3 mm); an axis already coarser, such as 2.5 mm or 5 mm slices, keeps its own spacing and its own values,
since resampling it would only add interpolated slices (from 2026-10-05, 01:52; the 420 copies made upsampled before
then were deleted and made again). It is kept as int16 Hounsfield units in NIfTI. Radiographs are kept with their longest side at 1024 px, and
mammograms at 2048 px, as 16-bit PNG. Then the DICOM files are deleted. A JSON file beside each copy keeps the
header fields a model or a split needs, plus the series' bucket folder and each object's md5, so any original can
be fetched again exactly as served.

Measured on the first five CT colonography series: 16.9 times smaller (2.0 GB to 0.12 GB); LDCT's low-dose scans
come out 10.6 times smaller. At 3,000 random points in each copy, the values matched an independent interpolation
of the original DICOM to within rounding (mean difference 0.0 HU), a series holding two passes included. Two
radiograph copies matched an independent area average of their source (correlation 0.9999 or better) and keep to
the source's own value range.

The licence is read from TCIA's own API on every run, and IDC's licence for each series is checked before that
series is fetched. A series under any other licence is never fetched.
